# Hot filter: the one-page pass

Read this page first. If a draft fails it, rewrite; the rest of the checks will not save it. The order is roughly how often each tell gave a model draft away in blind tests, where readers who knew the writer were asked to spot the fake.

1. **Contractions.** In casual writing, write "it's", "I've", "that's". A draft full of "it is" and "I have" reads like a press release, and it was the single most common giveaway.
2. **Match the energy of the moment.** No composed compliment closers ("Incredible effort, everyone!"), no press-release phrasing about personal news ("We are thrilled to embark on..."), no literary staging ("And in that quiet moment, everything shifted"). Say it the way the writer would say it on a call.
3. **One consequence chain at most.** "If X then Y", "X, so Y", "which means", "that's why": once per short piece. Two sentences in a row with the same logical shape is the tell. Some facts just sit there with no takeaway. Never fix a chain by chopping it into short period statements; that staccato is the same tell.
4. **Match the shape before the words.** Line breaks, paragraph length, where bold and emoji sit. A reader can spot a fake by its silhouette before reading a word. Match the writer's shape class, never one sample's exact skeleton.
5. **No dashes.** Zero em dashes, zero en dashes. Use commas, periods, parentheses.
6. **No minted lines.** No fresh aphorisms, no command-style maxim headers ("Ship small, learn fast"), no verbless wrap-up lines ("Three habits, one result."). A wise closing line is allowed only after a first-person story, and only if the writer would really say it.
7. **Shape bans.** No balanced A/B seesaws ("X does A, so let me do B"). No three-part flourishes made of abstractions ("faster, smarter, better"); a list of concrete things can be any length. No "not X but Y" in any form. No "A, not B." verdict fragments. No tidy list that ends in a twist.
8. **A ceiling on signature moves.** At most 2 of the writer's high-salience habits per short piece (per section on longer ones). A ceiling, never a target. Most real pieces carry 0 or 1.
9. **Numbers.** One or two numbers in a short post, written as digits, never a stat dump. "+" only on counts that really are open-ended; an exact figure stays exact.
10. **Say it aloud.** Every sentence must be something a person would say out loud. "The first time we tried it", never "attempt one". "Asking for help felt awkward", never "help-seeking carried a cost".
