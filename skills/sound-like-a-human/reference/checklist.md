# The checklist (run it on the visible draft, line by line)

Write the verdicts down in your working notes. A silent "make it better" pass is exactly the re-smoothing this skill exists to prevent.

1. **Ban pass.** Quote each sentence, title, header, label and caption, and test it against sections 1 to 4 of `ban-layer.md`. A skim misses things; go line by line.
2. **Dash scan.** Search the draft for em and en dashes. The count must be zero (unless the voice file says otherwise).
3. **Aphorism and symmetry scan.** Every line that sounds like wisdom is either the writer's own existing saying, quoted exactly, or a closing line that follows a first-person story. Kill balanced A/B lines, "X is Y until Z", "not just X but Y", "the whole reason I", and three-part flourishes.
4. **Consequence scan.** At most one "so" / "which means" / "if X then Y" / "that's why" chain per short piece, and never two sentences in a row with the same logical shape. Fix by flowing the thought into one sentence; never by chopping it up, never by deleting content.
5. **Signature-move budget.** Count the writer's high-salience habits (from the voice file). At most 2 per short piece or per section. Under budget is fine; never add one to reach it.
6. **Borrowed strings.** If the draft reuses 2 or more exact phrases quoted in the voice file as examples, it imitated the examples instead of the writer. Rewrite those lines in new words that do the same job.
7. **Facts.** Every claim and number about the writer traces to the writer or the voice file. Delete what does not. Then check every name, client, employer and personal detail against what the voice file says the writer never wants said; delete a match even when the writer supplied it in this request. Observations from the piece's own subject (what you measured, what happened) must be real and observed, never invented.
8. **Say it aloud.** Read each sentence as if on a call. Anything nobody would say gets the plain spoken version.
9. **Firsthand test** (posts and long-form). A take on a product carries one concrete detail of the writer actually using it. Thanks names what actually arrived and from whom.

If anything changed during this pass, run it again on the changed text. Stop at a pass with zero changes, normally the first or second.
