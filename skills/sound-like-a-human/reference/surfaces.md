# Surfaces: one voice, different dials

The voice is the same everywhere; each surface turns a few dials. Read ONLY the section for where this text will be published. The ban layer's sections 1 to 4 apply on every surface. When the writer's voice file has its own section for a surface, it wins over this one.

## Social posts (LinkedIn, X, Threads)

- The most emphatic dial: contractions always, exclamation marks where the excitement is real, emoji only at the moments that carry feeling.
- Open in the scene or on the point, never with a setup that promises a reveal.
- Real posts vary a lot in length. Do not settle every post into the same band.
- One or two numbers, from the writer's own experience.
- A closing question only when it is specific to the topic and the writer really wants the answers. Most posts just end.

## Long-form (articles, newsletters, chapters, build logs)

- Story first, lesson last. Never open with the lesson.
- Quantify the struggle before naming the feeling.
- Lessons sit within a sentence of a concrete number or event from the story.
- Transitions are plain time markers ("By then", "Since then", "Eventually"). Sentences may start with And, But, So.
- Titles may state what happened, answer included. Curiosity comes from the surprising fact, not from a teaser.
- Never invent a mentor, a chance meeting, or a quote to complete a story arc. Keep the beats that really happened.

## Slides

- Read in silence, so compress: headers carry the claim ("Churn doubled after the price change"), not a bare label ("Signups").
- Short bullets are fine here; prose paragraphs on a slide are not.
- One point per slide. If a slide needs two headers, it is two slides.
- Contractions can drop, but the ban layer still applies in full.

## Professional documents (memos, specs, proposals, reports, one-pagers)

- The first paragraph says what the reader must decide or know, and by when. Background comes after.
- Headers state findings ("Two of the three vendors miss the deadline"), never bare topics ("Vendor review").
- One document, one purpose. A second ask gets its own document or its own clearly labeled section.
- Define every acronym and internal name the first time it appears.
- Every number carries where it came from and when it was measured. Say plainly what is estimated.
- Comparisons go in a table, not in paragraphs.
- Say it once: no executive summary that the body then repeats, no conclusion that recaps.
- Plain words over formal ones ("use", never "utilize"; "about", never "with regard to"). Contractions are optional; the ban layer applies in full.

## Spoken (talk tracks, video scripts, podcast notes)

- Written to be heard once: shorter sentences than the page, repetition is allowed, signposts out loud ("Two things here").
- Every line must survive being said by the writer, in their own rhythm.

## Email and messages

- The plainest dial. The first sentence answers what was asked.
- No story-first openings in a reply, a form field or an application answer.
- Never quote someone's own marketing language back at them.
- Nothing touches the text after the final check; most failures here come from "one more tidy-up".
